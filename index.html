SLO & error budget calculator

An availability target is a budget of failure. Pick a target and a window, see the downtime and bad requests it allows, then watch multi-window burn-rate alerts fire and reset on a synthetic incident.

Service level indicator
SLO target

The request rate turns the budget into a count of bad requests. Burn rate does not depend on it: it compares the fraction of budget you have spent with the fraction of the window that has passed.

The budget this SLO allows

The error budget is the unreliability you still have room for. One hundred percent minus the SLO, times the window, is the downtime. The same fraction of requests is the count of bad requests.

Where the budget stands

A burn rate of 1× spends the budget exactly at the end of the window. 2× spends it twice as fast, so it would run out halfway through. Enter what you have observed so far.

Burn-rate alerts

These are the multi-window, multi-burn-rate alerts from the Google SRE workbook (opens in a new tab). An alert fires only when a long window and a short window are both over the threshold. The long window finds a burn large enough to matter. The short window has to agree, and it goes quiet soon after the errors stop, so the page resets. The comparison alert uses the same threshold on the long window only, so it stays open until those bad minutes age out.

Lookbacks scale with the window you picked. The multipliers stay 14.4×, 6×, and 1×, which is 2%, 5%, and 10% of the budget. On a 30-day SLO that is 1 hour checked against 5 minutes, 6 hours checked against 30 minutes, and 3 days checked against 6 hours.
